# Website

Static site published to <https://elyos-racing.github.io/team-portfolio/> by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` on every push to `main`.

```bash
pip install markdown pyyaml
python site/build.py                 # → _site/
python site/build.py --write-readme  # rewrite the README results table from data.yaml
python site/build.py --serve 8000    # build and preview with clean URLs, like GitHub Pages
```

Links have no `.html`: GitHub Pages serves `/mileage` from `mileage.html` and `/members/` from `members/index.html`. A plain `python -m http.server` does not, so preview with `--serve`.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `data.yaml` | Single source for the home page, the two competitions, the engineering areas, the headline numbers and the results table (README included); each result carries its `circuit`, and bars only compare results on the same one |
| `diagrams.py` | The hand-drawn diagram of each engineering page (see below) and the small glyphs of the home cards |
| `tracks.json` | Course outlines: Indianapolis Motor Speedway (Mileage) and Silesia Ring (ADC), from the official Shell Eco-marathon coordinates as processed by the team's track pipelines |
| `assets/` | Logo, favicon and share card, generated by `tools/brand_assets.py` from the official white logo |
| `style.css` | One dark theme for every page: tokens at the top, then layout, hero, components, Markdown pages, responsive rules and accessibility |
| `site.js` | Plain script, progressive: entrance gate (waits for Archivo, max 0.8 s, so text never jumps), scroll reveals, count-up numbers, results bars, the circuit hero, the diagram animations, the chapter rail and the lazy loader for the 3D car |
| `car3d.js` | 3D viewer (three.js from unpkg, loaded only when the car section gets near the screen): the 2026 car in x-ray (silver insides, translucent shell with a blue outline) on a floor grid drawn in the scene, turning slowly, drag to turn it; renders on demand and stops off screen. Data saver on: loads only on request |
| `assets/car.glb` | The 2026 car (also the ADC kit base), from the team's SolidWorks assembly exported to glTF and converted with gltf-transform: fasteners removed, simplified and meshopt-compressed (≈ 0.8 MB, 130 k triangles) |

### Circuit hero

The course outline is the hero on the home, Mileage and ADC pages. Its faint base line is there from the first paint (the stage has a fixed aspect ratio, so nothing shifts), the bright line draws itself once the font is in, and then a dot runs the course with a speed profile derived from the curvature of the official coordinates (lateral grip limit, smoothed). The readout shows status, lap, speed, distance and energy:

- **Home** (closed course): a time-lapse, the first lap at a readable pace and each one faster, until the kilowatt-hour runs out after 88 laps and 336 km, with the efficiency beside the energy left; then it stays on the result until Replay.
- **Mileage** (closed course): the race itself, 4 laps and 15.33 km as a steady time-lapse, with the race time against the 35-minute limit and the energy used (about 46 Wh at 336 km/kWh). The copy and the readout share the top row and the course takes the full width below.
- **ADC** (open course): one autonomous run from the start dot to the checkered finish line, which sits across the last stretch.

Once the outline has drawn itself, drafting marks appear around it, computed from the outline so they fit any course (`track_annotations()` in `build.py`): start/finish labels, the running direction, a length callout (the official 3,833 m lap at Indianapolis), a north arrow and a metre scale bar in the freest spot, and a handwritten note ("≈ 11 Wh a lap", "no hands from here"). The hero sits in a thin drawing frame with zone marks, and a hand-drawn swash underlines "88 laps.". Marks that would leave the drawing switch to the inner side of the track; they are hidden on phones.

It is plain SVG updated with `transform` and `stroke-dashoffset` (no WebGL, no library), it stops while off screen or in a hidden tab, the Pause motion button is remembered between visits, and with reduced motion it shows the finished result without moving.

### Desk and sheets

The page is a soft blueprint desk (mid blue with a faint white grid) between deep-navy header and footer bands that carry the official white logo; the hero, the headline numbers and the 3D car sit on the desk, and the content lies on it as drawing sheets (`sheet()` in `build.py`): cream graph paper, blue-pen ink, a drafting frame with zone marks (A–F, 1–4), an outlined sheet number, a dimension line that draws itself under the head, an optional handwritten margin note, highlighter on bold text and a CAD title block (drawing number `ELY-<code>-<nn>`, title, season, scale, sheet) at the foot. Type roles: Archivo for headings and text, JetBrains Mono for labels and data, Caveat only for handwritten notes. Ink on paper keeps at least 5.9:1 contrast.

### Member pages: the engineer's data sheet

Each `members/<user>.md` becomes a data sheet: a hand-drawn monogram (first letters of the first two names, or the optional `Initials` field) and identity block (role from the latest season heading, area, seasons, and GitHub and LinkedIn links with icons, also shown on the crew cards), then **Measured results** (optional table: Metric · Before · After · Unit; a row becomes before/after bars with the improvement written by hand, or one big number if Before is empty), **Revision history** (each `## Season YYYY · role` is a revision, REV A, B…), **Bill of skills** (the Skills paragraph as a numbered parts list) and **Worked on** (engineering pages matched from the Area field). The template explains the table.

### Engineering pages and their diagrams

`projects/*.md` are the portfolio of the car, one page per area (mechanics, electronics, motor driver, telemetry, energy strategy, autonomy), written from the team's repositories without code or design files. The first paragraph becomes the lede, then comes the diagram, then the rest of the Markdown. The order of `projects:` in `data.yaml` sets the home cards and the "Next" link at the end of each page.

Each diagram in `diagrams.py` is built from a few primitives (`box`, `arrow`, `path`, `circle`, `note`, plus the drafting helpers `center`, `dim` and `balloon` used by the mechanics sheet) on a 960 × 480 sheet. The look is an engineering notebook: graph paper, ink with a slight hand wobble drawn as geometry (each straight stroke becomes a gentle, seeded quadratic, so lines stay crisp; a displacement filter left nicks on long edges), handwritten notes in Caveat, and a CAD-style title block (figure, title, caption, scale). When the sheet scrolls into view every line draws itself in the order it was added, labels appear after their lines, and dots travel along the signal paths; the dots pause off screen, in a hidden tab, with the Pause motion button and with reduced motion. On phones the sheet keeps its size and scrolls sideways. The Autonomous page has its own sheet, the kit layout (`kit()`): where each part of the autonomy kit goes on the car, with item balloons and a parts list of what each connects to (part types only, no models), from the adc-stack README; parts still to be chosen are dashed. The Autonomy stack page keeps the software pipeline. To add one: write a function that returns a `Sheet`, register it in `SHEETS` and `CAPTIONS`, and give it a figure number in `FIGS` in `build.py`. Draw only what the project page states.

### Wide screens

From 1560 px a chapter rail sits in the left margin of the home and competition pages and highlights the section in view; from 1800 px the content column widens. Season and member pages put their summary in a sticky side panel from 1100 px.

## Accessibility

Skip link, `main`/`nav` landmarks, every section labelled by its heading, visible focus rings, 44 px minimum touch targets, text alternatives for the course outlines, the circuit animation and the 3D model (the live readout and the canvas are hidden from assistive technology, which gets a written description instead), a pause button for motion, reduced motion respected everywhere (no animation, final states shown), forced-colors support, and contrast of at least 4.5:1 for all text (muted text 7:1, signal blue 5.2:1 on the page background). If `site.js` fails to load, everything still shows after 2.5 s.

Type: Archivo, width 125 for headlines (echoes the extended ELYOS wordmark) and width 100 for text, with a metric-adjusted local fallback. Motion: expo-out easing, transform and opacity only.
